The best test: a live video call

  • Ask for a short live video call. Real people almost always agree within a few days. Constant excuses (broken camera, bad signal, deployed, on an oil rig) are the #1 catfish sign.
  • On the call, ask them to do something unpredictable: turn their head side-to-side, wave a hand in front of their face, hold up a specific number of fingers. Real-time deepfake filters often glitch on profile turns and hands crossing the face.
  • Watch for: frozen or blurry face while the background stays sharp, lips out of sync, the call "dropping" whenever you ask for movement, or a camera pointed at the ceiling.

Ask for a specific-pose selfie

Ask for a fresh selfie that couldn't have been pulled from someone else's account, for example:

  • Holding a piece of paper with your name and today's date
  • Touching their left ear with their right hand while giving a thumbs up
  • Pointing at a specific object you name (a spoon, a clock showing the time)

Heads-up: AI image tools can fake simple poses now. A live video call is still the strongest check.

Never, under any circumstances…

  • Send money, gift cards, crypto, or "fees" to someone you haven't met in person.
  • Invest on a platform or app they recommend ("my uncle's crypto strategy").
  • Share intimate photos. Sextortion scams often start with a fake profile.
  • Share bank logins, ID photos, or verification codes.

If you've been scammed in Canada, report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (in the US: FTC / IC3), and report the profile to the app.
